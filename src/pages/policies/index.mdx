# Policies and support

## Late work

Each assessment loses 5% of its weight per day late, up to five days, after
which it earns zero. The Hate Catalogue is the one exception: it's a running
document due at the end of week 3, and a late submission is marked as it
stands the day it's actually turned in, so it's never worth padding it after
the fact.

## Extensions

Ask before the due date, not after — email [Marisol](/people/marisol-quaye/)
with what's happened and how much time you need. Extensions are granted for
illness, misadventure or documented hardship, not for having started late.
A short extension for a good reason is easy; a long one needs paperwork the
university requires anyway.

## Academic integrity

Every one of this course's four assessments asks you to analyse and build
around a design you didn't create, which makes the line unusually clear:
your account of why something works has to be your own analysis, not a
paraphrase of someone else's write-up of the same interface. Citing a source
that pointed you at an example is fine and expected. Presenting someone
else's explanation of the mechanism as your own is not.

Use of AI tools is permitted for research, drafting and testing support,
provided the analysis and design decisions are yours and you can defend them
without the tool in the room — several assessments (the Hostile Design
Experiment in particular) require exactly that kind of live defence.

## Getting help

Workshop questions — anything about the interface or object you're currently
building — are fastest resolved live in a session with [Idris](/people/idris-fenn/),
in front of the thing itself. Course-level questions — extensions, grading
disputes, anything that can't wait until the next session — go to
[Marisol](/people/marisol-quaye/) by email. If a question about specific
feedback doesn't get resolved by the person who gave it, escalate it rather
than letting it sit.
