Airport security is slow, invasive and frustrating, and almost nobody would
call it badly designed. This week is the course's central twist: design
quality depends on what behaviour a system is trying to produce, and for
whom, not on how it feels to be on the receiving end of it. Bad experience
and bad design are different claims, and so are good experience and good
design — a distinction this week asks you to hold onto for the rest of the
semester, because it's the one the Redemption Project is built on.

## Outline

- design quality as "what behaviour is this trying to produce, and for whom"
- frustrating-but-necessary design: security, warnings, confirmations,
  interlocks
- Defend the Indefensible, [this week's session](/sessions/10-defend-the-indefensible/)
