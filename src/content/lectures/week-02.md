Someone designed the system in front of you, and someone else — not always
the person using it — benefits from the choices they made. A cancellation
process that takes fifteen minutes can be a genuine failure for the customer
and a genuine success for the business measuring how many people give up
partway through. Both descriptions are true at once, which is exactly why
"bad design" needs a second question attached to it: bad for whom?
