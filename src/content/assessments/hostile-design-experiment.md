## The brief

> Test your Worst Interface on real people, and find out whether it
> actually does what you built it to do.

Being annoying and being effective are different claims, and this is the
assessment that finally forces them apart. Recruit 3–5 participants, give
each the same task, and don't tell them which parts were deliberately
designed against them. Start with a specific question — does hiding the
free option behind a second menu make people more likely to pick the paid
one? — then watch, rather than ask, what actually happens: completion time,
wrong choices, hesitation, confusion, whatever's relevant to your interface.

## What you submit

A short report: the question you set out to answer, who you tested and how,
what happened, and an honest comparison between what you predicted in
Assessment 2 and what participants actually did. Somewhere around 800–1,000
words. A prediction that turns out wrong isn't a bad result here — the
report just has to show you can tell the difference between what you
expected and what the evidence says.
