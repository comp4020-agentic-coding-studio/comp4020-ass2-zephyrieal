## The brief

> Choose a real system you hate, work out why it's actually built that
> way, test it, then build and defend something better.

This isn't "here's a nicer UI." It's five moves on one real system — an
interface, a product, a service, a physical object, an administrative
process, whatever you can get real evidence about. First, the autopsy:
what's actually wrong with it, for whom, and why. Then the defence: the
legitimate reasons — cost, safety, law, accessibility, some stakeholder you
hadn't considered — that might explain why it was built this way anyway,
taken seriously rather than strawmanned. Then the experiment: some small
test or observation that shows the problem is real, not just annoying to
you personally. Then the redesign itself, which has to keep whatever
legitimate job the original was doing. And finally, a defence of that
redesign — why it's actually better, not just different, with evidence to
back the claim.

Scope this for twelve weeks, not twelve months. A small system done
properly beats an ambitious one done halfway.

## What you submit

A report of around 1,500–2,000 words carrying all five parts in order —
autopsy, defence, experiment, redesign, defence of the redesign — plus
whatever prototype fits your system: an interactive build, a redesigned
process, a service blueprint, a physical mock-up. It has to stand on its own
without you there to explain it. You'll also present it live at the Worst
Design Awards, which is really the same five parts compressed into: what it
was, why it existed, what the evidence showed, and what you changed.
